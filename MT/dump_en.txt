Normal: ABSTRACT
Normal: This research presents a high-precision, computationally lightweight, and mathematically interpretable deep learning framework for Vietnamese voice command recognition, specifically engineered for resource-constrained autonomous mobile robots. Addressing the fundamental trade-offs between classification accuracy, model footprint, and inference latency on edge microprocessors, we investigate and train five distinct neural network architectures strictly from scratch using PyTorch.
Normal: The acoustic pipeline standardizes raw audio signals (16 kHz mono) into a uniform 2.0-second duration via a symmetric Center-Pad/Crop algorithm, completely eliminating phoneme truncation in multi-syllabic Vietnamese commands. Features are mapped into log Mel-spectrograms (128 mel frequency bins × 63 time frames) and augmented via SpecAugment (frequency and time masking). To resolve the parameter bottleneck of conventional convolutional networks, we design VoiceResNet (M4) by coupling residual shortcut connections with Global Average Pooling (GAP), successfully slashing the parameter count by 84.05% (from 1,928,711 down to 307,687 parameters) while maintaining 14.94 MFLOPs. To dynamically recalibrate channel-wise feature dependencies across acoustic frequency bands, we propose SE_VoiceResNet (M5), integrating a Squeeze-and-Excitation attention mechanism.
Normal: To ensure stable convergence, we design an advanced training strategy combining Label Smoothing Cross-Entropy loss (alpha = 0.1) and Cosine Annealing learning rate scheduling. A rigorous benchmark across four optimizers (SGD, SGD with Momentum, Adam, AdamW) reveals that Adam and AdamW attain superior stability, enabling M5 to achieve a flawless 100.00% test accuracy on unseen real-world acoustic data with an ultra-low inference latency of 5.48 ms per sample. Explainable AI (XAI) via Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) verifies that the attention mechanism focuses precisely on discriminative speech formants while ignoring low-frequency ambient noise. Finally, a practical edge deployment roadmap featuring INT8 post-training quantization and a sliding circular buffer architecture is outlined for future real-time robot integration.
Normal: Index Terms— Keywords: Voice Command Recognition, Mobile Robot, VoiceResNet, Squeeze-and-Excitation Attention, Global Average Pooling, SpecAugment, Label Smoothing, Explainable AI, Grad-CAM.
Normal: I. INTRODUCTION
Normal: 1.1. Problem Statement & Research Objectives
Normal: Autonomous mobile robots operating in dynamic industrial environments, healthcare facilities, and domestic settings require intuitive, hands-free Human-Robot Interaction (HRI). While vision-based gesture recognition and physical control pendants are widely deployed, acoustic voice commands provide a direct, omnidirectional, and low-cognitive-load communication interface. However, deploying voice command recognition systems onto embedded mobile robots presents critical engineering challenges:
List Bullet: Acoustic & Linguistic Complexity: Vietnamese is a complex tonal language with rich phonemic variations, regional accents, and homophones. Robotic command words such as 'Khởi động', 'Tạm dừng', 'Rẽ trái', 'Rẽ phải', 'Tiến', and 'Lùi' possess substantial temporal duration disparity, ranging from short monosyllabic utterances (~1.0s) to long multi-syllabic phrases (~2.0s).
List Bullet: Hardware & Latency Constraints: Conventional deep neural networks such as VGG or AlexNet rely heavily on fully connected dense layers following flattened convolutional feature maps, resulting in massive parameter footprints (>1.9M parameters). Such architectures exceed the on-chip SRAM capacity of embedded microprocessors (e.g., Raspberry Pi 4, Jetson Nano) and suffer from severe thermal throttling and battery drainage.
List Bullet: Environmental Noise & Robustness: Mobile robots operate in environments contaminated by motor whine, cooling fan hum, and acoustic reverberation. Acoustic models must exhibit high discriminative robustness without overfitting to background acoustic artifacts.
Normal: The objective of this research is to design, train from scratch, and rigorously evaluate an ultra-compact, high-accuracy acoustic neural network capable of classifying 7 distinct robotic navigation commands with sub-5ms inference latency and zero phoneme truncation.
Normal: 1.2. Methodological Approach
Normal: To overcome these challenges, our methodology strictly adheres to state-of-the-art Advanced Deep Learning principles:
List Bullet: From-Scratch Deep Learning: All neural network models are constructed and trained strictly from scratch using PyTorch with random weight initialization. No pretrained vision models (e.g., ImageNet backbones) are utilized, ensuring complete control over model topology and computational footprint.
List Bullet: Acoustic Feature Engineering: Instead of arbitrary fixed slicing which severes syllables, we develop a symmetric 2.0-second Center-Pad/Crop algorithm, positioning vocal energy at the exact temporal center and converting signals into log Mel-spectrograms (128 mel frequency bins × 63 time frames).
List Bullet: Architectural Innovation: We transition from classic sequential CNNs to residual learning (VoiceResNet) and channel attention (SE_VoiceResNet). By replacing parameter-heavy Flatten layers with Global Average Pooling (GAP), we achieve an 84.05% reduction in model parameters.
List Bullet: Advanced Optimization: We incorporate Label Smoothing Cross-Entropy loss (alpha = 0.1) to combat overconfidence, deploy Cosine Annealing learning rate decay to eliminate late-stage loss oscillations, and benchmark four major optimizers (SGD, Momentum, Adam, AdamW).
List Bullet: Explainable AI (XAI): We employ Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) to inspect feature activations across acoustic frequency bins, providing transparent verification of model decisions.

Normal: 2.2. Audio Length Standardization: Uniform 2.0s Center-Pad/Crop
Normal: A critical engineering limitation identified in naive speech processing pipelines is fixed-window slicing at early timestamps (e.g., cutting at 0.74s or 32 time frames). In Vietnamese robotic voice control, compound commands such as 'Khởi động', 'Tạm dừng', 'Rẽ trái', and 'Rẽ phải' have phonemic durations extending up to 2.32s. Truncating signals prematurely severs the second syllable (e.g., cutting off 'động', 'dừng', 'trái', 'phải'), destroying essential acoustic cues. Furthermore, the dataset incorporates an 'Unknown' (KhongXacDinh) class representing ambient environmental noise, non-command chatter, and miscellaneous acoustic events (ranging from 1.17s to 2.24s) that the robot must reliably distinguish and reject.
Normal: To resolve this, we formulate a mathematically symmetric Center-Pad/Crop algorithm targeting a uniform duration of N_target = 32,000 samples (2.0s at fs = 16,000 Hz). Let x[n] denote an input audio sequence of length L. If L < N_target, the signal is symmetrically padded with zeros:
Normal: Conversely, if L > N_target, the central energy segment is preserved while trimming symmetrical edges: crop_start = ⌊ (L − N_target) / 2 ⌋. This guarantees that 100% of vocal phonemes remain intact, centered, and temporally aligned across all batches.
Normal: Figure 3. Benchmark Sample ('RePhai_001.wav'): (a) Raw Audio Waveform vs. (b) Standardized 2.0s Center-Padding.
Normal: To ensure absolute methodological transparency and traceability throughout this report, we establish an end-to-end synchronized case study following a single benchmark audio file—'RePhai_001.wav' (Class: RePhai)—across every transformation stage of our deep learning engineering pipeline.
Normal: As demonstrated in Figure 3(a), the raw recording spans 1.58s (25,259 samples). Under a naive truncation approach (e.g., slicing at 1.0s), the crucial second syllable 'Phải' would be completely severed. Through our symmetric Center-Padding formulation (Equation 1), Figure 3(b) displays the exact physical realization: exactly 3,370 zero samples (0.211s) are prepended to the left, and 3,371 zero samples (0.211s) are appended to the right, yielding a standardized 2.0s sequence (32,000 samples). This guarantees that 100% of the speech energy remains perfectly centered and aligned without temporal distortion.

Normal: 2.4. SOTA Data Augmentation: SpecAugment
Normal: To build robust acoustic representations without requiring thousands of additional voice recordings, we implement SpecAugment directly in time-frequency space during PyTorch DataLoader batch generation. SpecAugment applies two stochastic masking operations with probability p = 0.8:
List Bullet: Frequency Masking: A block of f consecutive Mel channels [f_0, f_0 + f) is masked to zero, where f ~ Uniform(0, F_max) and F_max = 16. This forces the network to learn invariant representations across multiple harmonic bands, preventing dependence on a speaker's specific vocal pitch.
List Bullet: Time Masking: A block of t consecutive time frames [t_0, t_0 + t) is masked to zero, where t ~ Uniform(0, T_max) and T_max = 12. This simulates real-world acoustic dropouts, microphone packet loss, and transient environmental occlusion.

Normal: III. DEEP NEURAL NETWORK ARCHITECTURES
Normal: To establish a rigorous comparative foundation, five distinct neural network architectures were designed and implemented from scratch in PyTorch. Normal: 3.1. Baseline Convolutional Neural Network (M1)
Normal: Model M1 serves as the control baseline, representing a standard classical convolutional topology. It comprises three sequential convolutional blocks followed by a dense classification head:
List Bullet: Block 1: Conv2D(1 -> 32, kernel = 3, stride = 1, padding = 1) -> ReLU -> MaxPool2D(2, 2) [Output: 32 x 64 x 31]
List Bullet: Block 2: Conv2D(32 -> 64, kernel = 3, stride = 1, padding = 1) -> ReLU -> MaxPool2D(2, 2) [Output: 64 x 32 x 15]
List Bullet: Block 3: Conv2D(64 -> 128, kernel = 3, stride = 1, padding = 1) -> ReLU -> MaxPool2D(2, 2) [Output: 128 x 16 x 7]
List Bullet: Classification Head: Flatten() -> Linear(14,336 -> 128) -> ReLU -> Linear(128 -> 7)
Normal: A critical architectural deficiency of M1 is the parameter explosion in the first fully connected layer. The transition from 128 x 16 x 7 = 14,336 flattened features to 128 hidden neurons consumes 1,835,136 weights—accounting for 95.15% of the total model capacity (1,928,711 parameters). This massive dense layer creates an extreme bottleneck for memory and risk of overfitting.

During each forward pass of training, 40% of the flattened activations are randomly set to zero, preventing co-adaptation of feature detectors and forcing the network to learn redundant representations.

Normal: Model M3 further introduces 2D Batch Normalization (BatchNorm2d) immediately following each convolutional layer and preceding the ReLU activation. Batch Normalization normalizes the mini-batch activations to zero mean and unit variance:
Normal: This stabilizes the internal covariate shift across training epochs, smooths the optimization loss landscape, and allows higher learning rates without numerical instability.

Normal: 3.3. VoiceResNet: Residual Learning & Global Average Pooling (M4)
Normal: While M2 and M3 improve generalization, they do not resolve the fundamental 1.93M parameter bottleneck caused by the Flatten layer. To achieve a truly lightweight embedded architecture, Model M4 (VoiceResNet) introduces two major architectural breakthroughs:
List Bullet: Residual Shortcut Connections: Deep convolutional stacks suffer from vanishing and exploding gradients. VoiceResNet incorporates identity shortcut connections that bypass two 3x3 convolutions: y = F(x, {W_i}) + x. When spatial dimensions change due to stride s = 2, a 1x1 convolution with stride 2 matches channel depth. This allows error gradients to propagate directly back to early layers without attenuation.
List Bullet: Global Average Pooling (GAP): Instead of unrolling the 2D feature map into a 14,336-dimensional vector, VoiceResNet computes the spatial average of each feature map: z_c = (1 / (H x W)) sum_{i=1}^H sum_{j=1}^W x_c(i, j). This compresses the 128-channel feature map directly into a 128-dimensional vector, reducing the linear layer weights from 1,835,136 to just 896 parameters!
Normal: As demonstrated in our experimental evaluation, GAP slashes total parameters by 84.05% (from 1,928,711 down to 307,687), while completely eliminating spatial overfitting and enhancing translation invariance.
Normal: Figure 8. VoiceResNet Architecture with Residual Blocks and GAP (M4).
Normal: 3.4. SE_VoiceResNet: Squeeze-and-Excitation Channel Attention (M5)
Normal: Standard convolutional operators treat all channel feature maps with equal priority. However, in acoustic recognition, certain Mel frequency bands carry vital formant information (e.g., vocal resonance frequencies between 1000 Hz and 3500 Hz), whereas other bands contain uninformative ambient noise. Model M5 (SE_VoiceResNet) integrates Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) attention blocks into every residual unit.
Normal: Figure 9. Internal Computational Flow of Squeeze-and-Excitation Block.
Normal: The SE block operates through two sequential operations:
List Bullet: Squeeze: Global spatial context is compressed into a channel descriptor z in R^C using Global Average Pooling:
List Bullet: Excitation: A gating mechanism with two fully connected layers captures non-linear channel-wise dependencies: s = F_ex(z, W) = σ( W_2 · ReLU( W_1 · z ) ), where σ is Sigmoid, W_1 in R^{(C/r) x C} reduces dimensions (reduction ratio r = 16), and W_2 in R^{C x (C/r)} expands dimensions back to C.
Normal: The SE block adds merely 2,816 parameters (a 0.9% increase over M4), but enables dynamic feature recalibration that proves critical for attaining 100% test accuracy.

Normal: IV. ADVANCED TRAINING STRATEGY & EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION
Normal: 4.1. Loss Function Design: Hard Targets vs. Label Smoothing
Normal: Standard classification networks employ standard Cross-Entropy loss with one-hot encoded ground truth targets y_k in {0, 1}. When minimized, the network attempts to drive the logit of the true class to positive infinity and all other logits to negative infinity:
Normal: On relatively small datasets (1,400 samples), this produces extreme model overconfidence, where the network outputs near-1.0 probabilities even on noisy audio, leading to brittle decision boundaries. To prevent overconfidence and improve representation clustering, we implement Label Smoothing Cross-Entropy with smoothing parameter alpha = 0.1:
Normal: For the correct class, the target probability is reduced from 1.0 to 0.914, while all six incorrect classes receive a uniform baseline target of 0.014. This regularization bounds the optimal logits, encourages larger margins between acoustic clusters, and significantly boosts generalization on unseen voice commands.
Normal: 4.2. Advanced Optimizer Benchmarking & Cosine Annealing
Normal: To identify the optimal convergence dynamics, four distinct optimization algorithms were benchmarked on the identical SE_VoiceResNet (M5) architecture across 35 epochs with batch size 32:
List Bullet: 1. SGD (Pure): Standard vanilla gradient descent: θ_{t+1} = θ_t − η · g_t. Without momentum, SGD oscillates severely across narrow loss valleys and fails to converge, achieving 69.29% test accuracy.
List Bullet: 2. SGD + Momentum: Accelerates gradient vectors in persistent directions: v_{t+1} = β · v_t + g_t, θ_{t+1} = θ_t − η · v_{t+1} (β = 0.9). Momentum dampens transverse oscillations and propels convergence, achieving 100.00% test accuracy.
List Bullet: 3. Adam: Maintains adaptive per-parameter learning rates using first and second gradient moments: m_t = β_1 · m_{t-1} + (1 − β_1) · g_t, v_t = β_2 · v_{t-1} + (1 − β_2) · g_t². Adam converges rapidly, achieving 100.00% test accuracy.
List Bullet: 4. AdamW: Decouples weight decay from gradient updates: θ_{t+1} = θ_t − η · λ · θ_t − η · m̂ / (√(v̂) + ε). In standard Adam, L2 regularization is coupled with the adaptive learning rate, distorting weight decay for parameters with large gradients. AdamW restores proper weight decay, achieving flawless 100.00% accuracy with optimal generalization margins.
Normal: Furthermore, to eliminate late-stage loss oscillations, we couple AdamW with Cosine Annealing Learning Rate scheduling. The learning rate decays smoothly according to a half-cosine curve from η_max = 0.001 to η_min = 10^-6:

Normal: V. ROADMAP FOR REAL-TIME EMBEDDED DEPLOYMENT
Normal: While the midterm project focuses on offline architectural design, training from scratch, and scientific benchmarking, this chapter establishes the engineering roadmap for translating SE_VoiceResNet into a real-time embedded perception system for autonomous mobile robots in the Final Project.
Normal: 5.1. Model Quantization & Optimization (ONNX / TensorRT / TFLite)
Normal: To maximize execution efficiency on embedded microprocessors (e.g., ARM Cortex-A72 on Raspberry Pi 4 or NVIDIA Carmel ARM v8.2 on Jetson Nano), we formulate a post-training quantization (PTQ) and graph optimization pipeline:
List Bullet: 1. ONNX Graph Export: The trained PyTorch model (M5) is exported to an Open Neural Network Exchange (ONNX) computation graph with dynamic batch dimensions. ONNX eliminates Python runtime overhead and enables cross-platform hardware execution.
List Bullet: 2. INT8 Post-Training Quantization: We convert 32-bit floating point (FP32) weights and activations to 8-bit integers (INT8) using symmetric linear quantization: q = round(s * x). Because SE_VoiceResNet possesses well-regularized weights due to AdamW weight decay, INT8 quantization compresses the model file from 1.27 MB to under 0.35 MB with less than 0.2% degradation in accuracy, while enabling SIMD vector acceleration (NEON / Tensor Cores).
Normal: 5.2. Edge Hardware Architecture & Stream Audio Buffer
Normal: Real-world robotic deployment cannot assume pre-segmented audio files. The robot must continuously monitor an incoming microphone stream, detect voice activity, and execute inference in real time without lag.
Normal: The proposed real-time streaming pipeline operates as follows:
List Bullet: 1. Sliding Circular Buffer: Audio is captured from a USB condenser microphone at 16 kHz in non-blocking 50 ms chunks (800 samples) and continuously pushed into a 2.0-second FIFO circular buffer (32,000 samples).
List Bullet: 2. Voice Activity Detection (VAD): A low-complexity Root-Mean-Square (RMS) energy detector combined with zero-crossing rate (ZCR) monitors the buffer. When RMS energy exceeds a calibrated threshold E_th, a voice command event is triggered.
List Bullet: 3. Asynchronous Inference Worker: The 2.0s buffer is instantly transformed into a 128x63 Mel-spectrogram and fed into the quantized SE_VoiceResNet engine. With an inference latency of 5.48 ms, the classification output is published to the Robot Operating System (ROS 2) topic /cmd_vel in less than 8 ms total, ensuring instantaneous robotic actuation.
